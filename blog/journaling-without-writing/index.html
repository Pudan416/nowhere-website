Journaling without writing: keep a record of your day

By Kosta Pudan

You get to the end of a full day and can't tell what was in it. Work. Messages. Something for lunch. Probably a few things worth keeping.

Then you open a journal. An empty page. Another thing to write.

A journal can be a photo, a voice note, or a picture made from the moments you lived. You don't have to explain the whole day to keep a piece of it.

Keep one photo

The table after dinner. Something half-finished on your desk. The light at the bus stop.

Choose a photo because it brings back a moment, even if it is a terrible photograph. Put it in a dedicated album so you can find it again.

Add a few words if it needs context. “Walk after the rain” is enough.

A photo keeps what was visible. If the part you care about was a conversation, try this instead.

Say it out loud

Open the recorder on your phone and finish this sentence:

The thing I want to remember from today is…

Say what happened and why you chose it. Stop when you have said enough. No clean take. No need to sound like you have started a podcast.

Give the recording a name you will recognize. Otherwise, finding the right “New Recording” a month later becomes its own project.

Give the day a picture

A walk. Coffee. A meal with someone. You can record those moments with shapes, colors, or symbols instead of paragraphs.

On paper, a circle could stand for coffee with a friend. In a visual diary, the activities you choose can become part of a picture.

That is how Nowhere works: you choose the moments you lived, and they shape your daily Canvas and its music.

An abstract image won't keep the exact words someone said. If those matter, keep a note or recording alongside it.

Looking back can be visual too. This is an example day poster from Nowhere, with the day's image and recorded activities together.

Keep one thing today

Pick one format for your next entry. You can try the others another time.

After a few entries, open them. Can you tell the days apart? Does anything bring back a detail? If the photos all look the same, add a caption. If you never listen to the recordings, try something you can see at a glance.

I wrote about days that felt full but left little to remember in Work-Life Balance Is Broken. There is also a small noticing experiment if you want somewhere to start.

For today, what is one thing you would like to keep?
